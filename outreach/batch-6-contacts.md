### Rules that are set-cover, not sums

The strongest structural line available, and it now has five independent instances:

- **UJ** — Informatyka II st. needs **6 of 10** electives matching the chosen ścieżka. Exactly 120 ECTS can still fail.
- **AGH WI** — one semester-2 elective must be English-taught. A perfect 30/30 elective ECTS can still fail.
- **Bonn** — 88 LP Wahlpflicht with six interlocking rules: 31–61 LP Schwerpunkt containing ≥1 seminar *and* ≥1 lab, two further areas ≥6 LP, caps of 10 LP seminars / 18 LP labs.
- **Lund LTH** — specialisation courses must all sit in **one and the same läro- och timplan**.
- **Copenhagen** — the three overlapping restricted-elective lists above.

### Poland: the USOS claim, now precise

Three independent agents refined the Warsaw finding, and the earlier wording was too strong. **Do not
write that USOS "cannot" check plans.** The accurate claim is about *stage*, not capability:

- USOS validates **each course** — PW's WEiTI rejects declarations with missing prerequisites at registration.
- USOS never validates **the set** against the degree.
- USOS *does* ship a staff-side "Rozliczanie studentów z wymagań programu studiów" module (confirmed at UJ) — it runs after the session, on already-graded courses, and a human in the dziekanat signs off.
- **AGH runs USOS after all** (`web.usos.agh.edu.pl`); Wirtualna Uczelnia is a portal on top, not a replacement. My brief was wrong about this.

**The Warsaw drafts (#79–82, Batch 5) predate this refinement — re-read them against this wording

# 1. Politechnika Warszawska

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

The USOS finding is **confirmed for PW, with one important refinement — PW's WEiTI does more at registration than UW's MIMUW does, and it is worth knowing exactly what.**

WEiTI migrated course registration into **USOSweb** (the 2025/26 and 2026/27 procedure notices point students at `usosweb.usos.pw.edu.pl/kontroler.php?_action=news/rejestracje/rejJednostki&jed_org_kod=103000`). The mechanism is a **deklaracja semestralna**: in the last weeks of each semester the student submits the *set* of courses they want next semester; then six automated allocation draws (23–30 September 2026, 14:50–18:00) assign places.
Source: https://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/Aktualnosci/Komunikaty-Dziekanatu/Procedura-zapisow-na-przedmioty-realizowane-w-semestrze-zimowym-2026-2027

What the allocation actually enforces, per the faculty's own rules page:
- **seat limits** (`limitów miejsc na przedmioty`, dean may adjust after a trial registration);
- **per-subject formal admissibility** — students are dropped from the draw for a course if *"deklaracje, w odniesieniu do tego przedmiotu, zawierają błędy formalne (np. brak wymaganych poprzedników)"*;
- an empty/unsubmitted declaration *"odpowiada złożeniu deklaracji pustej, co oznacza deklarację nieuczęszczania"*.
Source: http://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/pol/Studenci/Zalaczniki/Zapisy-na-przedmioty2/Zasady-zapisow-na-przedmioty

So **PW does check prerequisites at declaration time** — MIMUW's USOS did not. That is a real difference and the brief's blanket "USOS does no plan validation" is too strong here.

What it still does **not** do — and this is the whole opening:
- it validates each course *individually* (has this student got the poprzedniki?), never the **set** against the degree's ECTS-per-class-of-subjects structure;
- the degree-level check is a **semestral arithmetic progress control** (`rygory studiowania`: ≥20/40/60/80/100/120 cumulative ECTS to register for semesters 1–6) run *after* grades exist;
- plus a second structural rule almost nobody can eyeball: from semester 3, **delay per class of subjects may not exceed two semesters relative to the model plan**. That is a per-group timing constraint checked by a human, not by USOS.
Source: https://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/layout/set/print/Studenci/Informacje-o-studiach/Studia-drugiego-stopnia/Wymagania-plany-rygory/Rygory-studiowania2

**The human approval step is real and it is written down:**
> *"Studenci, którzy mają opiekunów naukowych, mają obowiązek uzgodnienia deklaracji semestralnych ze swoimi opiekunami."*
Source: http://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/pol/Studenci/Zalaczniki/Zapisy-na-przedmioty2/Zasady-zapisow-na-przedmioty

And an opiekun naukowy is **mandatory** for every 2nd-cycle student (chosen by week 5 of sem. 2 on 4-sem programmes, sem. 1 on 3-sem programmes; assigned via APD, approved by a per-specialisation commission chaired by the institutes' Zastępcy Dyrektora ds. nauczania).
Source: https://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/Studenci/Informacje-o-studiach/Studia-drugiego-stopnia/Zalozenia-organizacyjne-i-programowe2/Opiekunowie-naukowi

**No PW-specific validator exists on top of USOS.** WEiTI has a `Pełnomocnik Dziekana ds. międzynarodowej wymiany studentów i rozwoju systemu USOS` (dr inż. Dariusz Turlej, dariusz.turlej@pw.edu.pl) — i.e. USOS development is a faculty portfolio, so there is an owner who would know if anything else existed, and nothing else is published. The only adjacent student-built thing is `weiti.pw`, a **course-rating/opinion** site (Facebook: systemweitipw) — it helps students judge *whether a course is good*, not *whether their set is legal*. Complementary, not competing.

**Corrections to the brief:** several, and two of them change the pitch.

1. **"Data Science" is not an EiTI programme.** There is no Data Science MSc at WEiTI. It lives at **MiNI** as *Inżynieria i Analiza Danych* (English-taught as "Data Science"). Pitching Data Science to EiTI would have been an immediate own-goal.
   Sources: https://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/Kandydaci/Stacjonarne-studia-drugiego-stopnia/Kierunki-studiow-i-specjalnosci · https://www.pw.edu.pl/studia/studia-ii-stopnia/inzynieria-i-analiza-danych-data-science

2. **Most WEiTI master's are 3 semesters / 90 ECTS, not 4 / 120.** Only *Informatyka* and *Automatyka i Robotyka* are 4-semester/120 ECTS (AiR also offered as 3-sem). *Telekomunikacja*, *Elektronika*, *Cyberbezpieczeństwo*, *Inżynieria Internetu Rzeczy* are 3-semester. Any draft that assumes 120 ECTS across the board is wrong for four of seven kierunki.

3. **"Compulsory vs elective (obowiązkowe / obieralne)" is the wrong model for WEiTI.** WEiTI does not express requirements that way. It expresses them as **ECTS minima per klasa przedmiotów**. For a 4-semester MSc, 120 ECTS =
   - przedmioty **HES** (ekonomiczno-społeczne): **6 ECTS**
   - przedmioty **techniczne**: **82 ECTS**, itself split **30 ECTS podstawowe dla specjalności + 52 ECTS zaawansowane**
   - **praca dyplomowa**: **32 ECTS**
   - **język obcy**: 0 ECTS but the exam is compulsory — a zero-credit requirement a pure ECTS-sum validator would silently miss.
   Source: https://www.elka.pw.edu.pl/Studenci/Informacje-o-studiach/Studia-drugiego-stopnia/Wymagania-plany-rygory/Wymagania-programowe

4. **"Specialisation requirements" don't apply to every programme.** *Automatyka i Robotyka*, *Cyberbezpieczeństwo* and *Inżynieria Internetu Rzeczy* have **no specjalności at all**. Where specjalności exist, they are chosen **at admission**, not mid-degree — e.g. Informatyka: *Informatyka w Multimediach*, *Inteligentne Systemy*, *Sztuczna Inteligencja*. Requirements differ between specjalności **only inside the technical block**, with the class totals held constant.

|---|---|---|
| USOSweb | web.usos.agh.edu.pl | Course registration (żetony/tury), grades, APD, catalogue |

# 4. Sorbonne Université

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

Sorbonne is **not** Paris-Saclay. Two things are true at once, and both matter.

**(a) The contrat pédagogique exists and is binding — but its teeth are different.**
Sorbonne's Faculté des Sciences et Ingénierie MCC (règlement master) says, Article 2 – *Inscription administrative et pédagogique*:

> « Lors de son inscription pédagogique, en concertation avec le département de formation dont il dépend, l'étudiant définit son contrat pédagogique, c'est-à-dire :
> – Les UE constitutives du (des) semestre(s) pédagogique(s) de son parcours de formation et sur lesquelles portera la compensation (30 crédits).
> – Les UE dont il suivra les enseignements durant la période. »

Article 4: « Chaque semestre pédagogique correspond à 30 crédits. 4 Semestres de 30 crédits soit 120 crédits au total sont nécessaires pour valider un Master. » and « La composition des semestres du parcours de formation est définie par l'étudiant au fur et à mesure de sa progression, en concertation avec le(s) département(s) de formation dont il (elle) dépend. Elle est déterminée en précisant les UE du contrat pédagogique prises parmi les UE prévues dans la maquette d'accréditation. »

Article 9 – *Compensations* is the killer clause:

> « Toutes les UE inscrites dans le contrat semestriel pédagogique participent à la compensation. Dans les cas où l'étudiant […] a préparé pendant cette période des UE pour plus de 30 crédits, la compensation se fait alors sur les 30 crédits du contrat semestriel pédagogique, **les UE supplémentaires non prévues au contrat semestriel pédagogique n'entrent pas dans la compensation.** »

Article 12 – *Réorientation et changements de parcours*: « Si dans cette nouvelle période il change d'UE libre ou optionnelle, la compensation ne peut se faire qu'avec la note de l'UE choisie dans la nouvelle période. »
Article 16: the jury de diplôme meets at the **end** of the master and decerns the parcours « en vérifiant que l'ensemble du parcours satisfait les exigences définies dans le document d'accréditation. »

# 5. University of Copenhagen (DIKU)

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

KU Faculty of Science runs its own planner: the **SCIENCE Course Plan / "studieplan"** at
`https://studenterservice.science.ku.dk/studieplan/?lang=en`. No login, no KUnet account, public URL.
It lets a student lay out study years, blocks (1–5), schedule groups (A–E), and tag each course with a
type dropdown — **Obligatory / Limited elective / Elective** — and a status (Not begun / Passed / Not passed).
Its only aggregate is a single running counter, rendered in the page template as literally
`Antal ECTS-point: {{ects_total}}`. The page carries no statement that it validates against a
studieordning, and it is not binding or submitted anywhere. The categories are self-assigned labels;
nothing checks that the courses you tagged "Limited elective" actually satisfy the curriculum's lists.
It is a categorised ECTS adder — and that is exactly what it was built to be.
Source: https://studenterservice.science.ku.dk/studieplan/?lang=en

**What actually gets validated, and when.** Registration goes through **Self Service on KUnet** in fixed
windows (ordinary: 15 Nov–1 Dec and 15 May–1 Jun; late-registration windows per block). SCIENCE's
*Course and exam rules 2025-26*, clause **2.6 "Registration for courses and projects which cannot be
included in the student's degree programme"**, says verbatim:

> "If, during an ordinary registration period, students have registered for too many ECTS credits in
> relation to their curriculum, they will be withdrawn from the excess course(s)/project(s) by the
> faculty and be notified thereof via KUmail."
> "If students have registered for courses or projects which cannot be included in their degree
> programme and have not applied for or have not been granted a required dispensation, their
> registration for the course will also be withdrawn by the faculty, and the students will be notified
> thereof via KUmail."
> "If students register for courses or projects in the late-registration period and are sent a receipt in
> their KUmail, stating that their registration has been rejected … The students must contact Student
> Services for further clarification."

So KU **does** enforce, unlike DTU's planner — but on exactly two axes: (a) total ECTS over the
curriculum's ceiling, and (b) a course that cannot be included in the programme *at all*. It runs
retroactively, after the window closes, and reaches the student as a silent withdrawal or a rejected
receipt in KUmail. It does **not** check how the 60 restricted-elective ECTS are allocated across the
curriculum's three lists. Source (PDF, clauses 2.1–2.8, 4.1–4.3, 17):
https://science.ku.dk/studerende/studieordninger/undervisnings-og-eksamensregler/ENG_Undervisnings-_og_eksamensregler.pdf

**Formal approval steps that do exist at KU:** a **thesis contract** created in Self Service and pushed to
status 'ready for approval' → 'Approved' (clause 3.3); **dispensation** from the Study Board (curriculum
§7: "In exceptional circumstances, the study board may grant exemptions from the rules in the curriculum");
and **pre-approval (forhåndsgodkendelse) + credit transfer** for academic mobility (curriculum §6.1.5).
Individual course selections are never submitted for approval.

**Corrections to the brief:**

1. **"Compulsory vs restricted-elective vs free-elective blocks" is wrong for KU's MSc Computer Science —
   the compulsory block is empty.** Curriculum §6.1 gives the whole programme as:
   *Restricted elective subject elements 60 ECTS; Elective subject elements 30 ECTS (thesis 30) or 15 ECTS
   (thesis 45); Thesis 30 or 45 ECTS.* There are **zero compulsory courses**. Nothing resembling DTU's
   10/50/30/30 applies. (Advanced Programming / AADS / Advanced Computer Systems *were* compulsory under
   the 2024/25 interim arrangement — Appendix 2 shows them shaded as fixed — and were converted into
   restricted-elective list 1 for 2025/26 onward. So the "compulsory" framing was true two years ago and
   is not true now.)

2. **The "eight study tracks" advertised on ku.dk are not specialisations and carry no regulatory force.**
   The curriculum says flatly, §2.3: *"There are no defined specialisations in this programme."* The
   marketing page listing Algorithms and Complexity, Machine Learning, HCI, DADIU etc. is advisory course
   bundling only. I would have got this wrong from the public page alone.

3. **The restricted-elective lists overlap, which is the real structural problem.** The 60 ECTS must be
   covered as 15 ECTS from list 1, 7.5 ECTS from list 2, and 37.5 ECTS from list 3 — but **the same course
   appears in two lists**. *Advanced Programming* (NDAA09013U) is on list 1 **and** list 3. *Machine
   Learning A*, *Natural Language Processing*, *Deep Learning*, *Advanced Topics in Deep Learning*,
   *Online and Reinforcement Learning* and *Machine Learning B* are all on list 2 **and** list 3.
   A course counts once. So a student can hold 60 ECTS of genuinely valid restricted electives and still
   fail the 15/7.5/37.5 allocation. This is a bipartite assignment problem, not a sum — and nothing at KU
   checks it before graduation.

4. **"Submitted for approval" describes nothing real for course choices at KU.** See above — thesis
   contract, dispensation, and mobility pre-approval are the only approval gates. There is no
   studieplan-godkendelse.

5. **MSc IT and Cognition is not a DIKU or Faculty of Science programme.** It sits at the **Faculty of

   elective, specialisation courses, 30 ECTS thesis). The brief grouped it under DIKU; it isn't.
   Source: https://www.ku.dk/studies/masters/it-and-cognition

6. **MSc Bioinformatics is the one that fits the brief's original shape.** 37.5 compulsory / 37.5 restricted
   elective / 15 elective / 30 thesis, hosted at **Biologisk Institut** (not DIKU), with **two real
   specialisations** — Computational Biology and Computer Science — chosen *before* the programme starts,
   with hard deadlines (1 April / 1 October) and a documented change procedure. Clause 4.3 requires that
   passed courses, approved credits, binding registrations **and binding pre-approvals** must all fit the
   target specialisation before a change is granted. Clause 4.2 auto-assigns a specialisation to students
   who miss the deadline.
   Source: https://www.ku.dk/studies/masters/bioinformatics

7. **DTU overlap is real and documented.** The MSc CS curriculum §4.1 lists *"Software Technology from
   Technical University of Denmark"* among the bachelor degrees that automatically fulfil the academic
   requirements. There is a live DTU→KU pipeline, both institutions are in Copenhagen, and DTU is already

8. Minor: elective ECTS at KU are not free-for-all. Curriculum §6.1.2 caps BSc-level subject elements at
   15 ECTS; §6.1.3 caps PUK (projects outside course scope) at 15 ECTS across restricted+elective,
   PIP at 15 ECTS, PREP at 15 ECTS, and PUK+PIP combined at 30 ECTS of the programme. Four more ceilings

# 6. Ghent University

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

Ghent is *not* KU Leuven. The KU Leuven finding does **not** transfer.

What Oasis actually does, from official sources:

1. **It totals credits, it does not judge them.** The official UGent step-by-step deck (Faculteit Psychologie en Pedagogische Wetenschappen, "STAPPENPLAN CURRICULUM SAMENSTELLEN", slide 11) reproduces the live Oasis curriculum editor. The only rule-like feedback on screen is two running counters: `studiepunten opgenomen in opleiding: 38` and `totaal aantal studiepunten opgenomen in dit academiejaar: 38`. Courses are added and removed with `+Toevoegen` / `– Weglaten` from programme modules. There is no pass/fail check against the programme's composition rules, no "you are 3 credits short", no "you exceeded the cap on this list".
   Source: https://www.ugent.be/pp/nl/student/stappenplancurriculum (PDF, slides 10–17)
2. **Strict prerequisites *are* enforced by the system.** Faculty of Sciences: "Het is niet mogelijk om vakken op te nemen in je curriculum waarbij je ingaat tegen deze volgtijdelijkheid" — but only for *strikte* volgtijdelijkheid. *Geadviseerde* volgtijdelijkheid "heeft **geen bindend karakter**".
   Source: https://www.ugent.be/we/nl/onderwijs/monitoraat/volgtijdelijkheid.htm
3. **The faculty rules live in a separate PDF and the student is explicitly made responsible for applying them.** FEA's own "CURRICULUMREGELS ACADEMIEJAAR 2026-2027" (9 pages) §6: *"De student is zelf verantwoordelijk voor de keuze van zijn opleidingsonderdelen bij de opmaak van het curriculum. […] Studenten die een opleidingsonderdeel uit een hoger modeltrajectjaar willen opnemen, kijken eerst in de studiefiche na of ze voldoen aan de vooropgestelde begincompetenties."* The binding prerequisites are printed as four tables at the back of that PDF (Tabel 1.a, 1.b, 2, 3), not surfaced in the tool.
   Source: https://www.ugent.be/ea/nl/diensten/studieondersteuning/trajectbegeleiding/curriculumregels2025_nl.pdf
4. **Review is manual, individual, and slow.** *"Jouw curriculumvoorstel wordt individueel nagekeken. De goedkeuring van je curriculum kan daardoor enkele weken op zich laten wachten."* If rejected: *"krijg je een mail met de vraag je curriculum aan te passen."*
   Source: stappenplan PDF, slides 16–17.
5. **The student gets one shot.** *"Je kan als student slechts één curriculumvoorstel per academiejaar indienen, nadien kan je zelf niks meer aanpassen."* After submission, changes go through a form to the programme / trajectbegeleider and must be approved by the curriculumcommissie.
   Source: stappenplan PDF, slide 14; FEA curriculumregels §11.

**Net:** Oasis is a correct submission-and-administration system with hard registration guards. It is not a plan validator, and UGent does not claim it is. That is exactly the gap StudyPlanner sits in — and unlike KU Leuven, no institutional workaround (no SharePoint sheet, no FAQ of rejection causes) is published, because the published answer is "read the 9-page PDF and the studiefiches yourself".

---

**Corrections to the brief:**

Five, and three of them matter.

1. **The deadline is 30 September, not mid-October.** FEA's 2026-2027 curriculumregels §2: *"Elk curriculum moet voorgelegd worden ter goedkeuring via Oasis uiterlijk op **30 september 2026**."* The KU Leuven "mid-October closes the window" assumption is two weeks too generous here. Today is 27 August 2026 — the window is open now and shuts in roughly five weeks.
2. **There is a brutal timing squeeze the brief did not anticipate, and it is the real hook.** Submission closes 30 September. The Curriculumcommissie only has to notify the student *"uiterlijk vóór 15 november"*. But a change to a first-semester course must be requested *"uiterlijk 14 november"*. A student who finds out on 12 November that their curriculum was adjusted has two days to react — and the first-semester exams start in January. Both dates are in the same faculty PDF, §2.
3. **MSc Electrical Engineering has essentially nothing to validate — drop it.** Studiekiezer lists the programme as 96 ECTS of "Algemene opleidingsonderdelen" plus a 24 ECTS masterproef. No keuzevak block, no `Op te nemen` line, no afstudeerrichtingen. Verified on two URLs. A study-plan validator has no job here. This is the weakest of the four targets and I did not build a contact around it.
4. **Master of Computer Science (Faculty of Sciences) is taught in Dutch, not English.** Studiekiezer returns *"This programme is taught in Dutch. Deze opleiding heeft als onderwijstaal Nederlands."* It is "Master of Science in de informatica". Structure: 120 ECTS = 60 core + 30 masterproef + a 30 ECTS **minor**, chosen as `Op te nemen: 1 minor uit de onderstaande lijst` between **Minor Onderzoek** and **Minor Economie en Bedrijfskunde**, then `30 studiepunten uit 1 tot 2 modules`, with `hoogstens 6 studiepunten` from other UGent programmes. That nesting — pick a minor, then modules within it, then caps within those — is the most validator-shaped structure of the four.
5. **Information Engineering Technology is a 60 ECTS one-year master, not a 120 ECTS two-year one.** 27 ECTS compulsory + 18 ECTS masterproef + 15 ECTS electives, and the elective block itself is three nested rules: `3 tot 15 studiepunten te selecteren uit de onderstaande lijst. Na goedkeuring door de faculteit`, `hoogstens 6 studiepunten` internship, `hoogstens 6 studiepunten` UGent-wide. Also Dutch-taught with some English courses.

Bonus, on approval authority: **the Curriculumcommissie approves, not the opleidingscommissie.** But a free elective from an open list needs a three-way sign-off — *"enkel na goedkeuring van de curriculumcommissie (na akkoord van de verantwoordelijk lesgever van het vak en van de opleidingscommissievoorzitter)"* — and the student routes all of it through the **facultaire trajectbegeleider**. §11: any deviation must be discussed with the trajectbegeleider ***vooraf*, before the proposal is submitted in Oasis.** §13 names the trajectbegeleiders as the faculty's single point of contact for curriculum questions. That makes the trajectbegeleider, not the commission and not the programme chair, the right recipient.

---

**Verdict: SEND. High priority — the strongest target since the campaign started.**

Reasons: (a) the incumbent genuinely does not do this job and nobody at UGent claims otherwise; (b) the rules are published as a PDF plus per-course studiefiches, which is precisely the input a validator consumes; (c) the student explicitly gets **one** submission and then loses edit rights; (d) the trajectbegeleider is a named, reachable, role-inboxed owner whose workload is directly reduced; (e) the calendar is live right now.

The only weak leg is Electrical Engineering (correction 3) — excluded.

**Hook:**

*One shot, then six weeks of silence.* A UGent master's student assembles their curriculum in Oasis, which shows them a credit counter and nothing else, submits it once by 30 September, cannot edit it afterwards, and may not hear the verdict until 14 November — the same day the deadline to change a first-semester course expires. StudyPlanner runs the faculty's own composition rules against the plan *before* that single submission, so what arrives at the trajectbegeleider is already internally consistent.

Framing discipline: Oasis is described as doing its job correctly at submission and administration. StudyPlanner is positioned strictly upstream of the `Voorleggen ter goedkeuring` button. Never a replacement.

# 7. Sapienza Università di Roma

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

Sapienza's official term is **"percorso formativo"**, not "piano di studi" (both appear, but the catalogue and the university page use *percorso formativo*).

- **Front door = Infostud.** Official university page states verbatim: *"Il percorso formativo (o piano di studio), se previsto nel regolamento del tuo corso, si compila su Infostud."* (https://www.uniroma1.it/it/content/percorso-formativo)
- **Back end = GOMP.** DIAG's own instructions say students submit *"attraverso la procedura Percorsi formativi/GOMP/INFOSTUD"*. After approval there is a documented **24–48h sync lag** before Infostud shows the new courses. So it is a two-system pipeline, not one system.
- **What it actually validates:** CFU arithmetic and eligibility to book exams. The Computer Science LM-18 page states: *"The Infostud system forbids students to book and clear an exam which is not included in their study plan currently in force."* Structural correctness (group quotas, coherence) is **not** checked at compile time — it is checked later, by humans.
- **Curricolare vs individuale — the brief's "statutario" is the wrong word.** Sapienza distinguishes **"percorso formativo curricolare"** (approved automatically when the choices are *"coerenti e compatibili"*) from **"percorso formativo individuale"** (goes to the CAD for evaluation at its next session). Guidance to students literally reads: *"se la pagina propone 'percorso formativo individuale', non selezionarlo ma scegli 'Curricolare'."*
- **Approving body = the CAD (Consiglio di Area Didattica)**, not a "Commissione Piani di Studio". Each CAD has a Presidente and several delegate a named **"responsabile dei piani di studio"**.

Sources: https://www.uniroma1.it/it/content/percorso-formativo · https://corsidilaurea.uniroma1.it/it/course/33515/attendance/instructions · https://corsidilaurea.uniroma1.it/en/course/33508/attendance/instructions · https://corsidilaurea.uniroma1.it/it/course/33503/attendance/instructions

# 8. University of Twente

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

Twente does **not** work like Radboud and does **not** work like UvA. Neither of the established findings transfers. Twente's mechanism is a **delegated human approver called the "Programme mentor"**, defined faculty-wide across EEMCS.

CS EER 2025-2026, definitions (Art. B1, p.27): *"Programme mentor: individual mandated by the Examination Board to approve course programmes for their specific specialisation."*

Art. B4.2 *Course programme approval*:
> "1. Every student is required to obtain course programme approval from the Programme mentor of their selected specialisation.
> 2. The programme approval is an agreement on the content of a student's individual course programme between the student and the Programme mentor. **The Programme mentor approves a programme on behalf of the Examination Board.**
> 3. Students are allowed to complete courses and sit examinations **up to a maximum of 15 credits** in a specialisation **before contacting the Programme mentor** for an approved course programme. After 15 credits permission from the Programme mentor is required for complete programme of 120 credits."

Source: https://www.utwente.nl/en/eemcs/education/rules-guidelines/eer-m/eer-m-2025/m-eer-25-26-m-cs.pdf

So: the Examination Board itself is **not** the first-line approver for CS/EE/EmSys/I-Tech — it has delegated that to individual academics per specialisation. The Board is the *escalation* path (Art. B4.1 §6: *"Exceptions to the composition of the programme can be approved by the Examination Board."*).

**The deadline is unusually early.** CS programme-approval page: students should have the programme established and mentor-approved *"not later than two weeks before the end of the first quartile."* Source: https://www.utwente.nl/en/cs/programme/programme-approval/

**The actual tool is a per-cohort, per-specialisation web/Excel/Word form.** https://www.utwente.nl/en/cs/forms/ hosts a separate form for every cohort year × specialisation × EIT entry/exit variant — `m-cs-cybsec-24-25.xlsx`, `2025-m-cs-cybsec-twente.xlsx`, `2025-m-cs-cybsec-eit-entry.docx`, plus WebHare web forms `st-25-26/`, `m-cs-dst-25-26/`, `m-cs-ist-25-26/`, `st-26-27/`, `susco-26-27/`. CybSec forms are hosted off-site at `4tu.nl/cybsec/course-program/`.

**Key verified detail (the hook).** The ST 2025-26 web form (https://www.utwente.nl/en/cs/forms/2025-2026/st-25-26/) is a real WebHare submission form with 32 course checkboxes and a Submit button. Its "Total credits" field is raw HTML:
```
<input ... type="number" ... placeholder="120" required min="120" max="240">
```
The total is **typed in by the student**; the form does not sum the ticked checkboxes (no calculation script — the only JS is the site-wide `utwente_base.ws2016/ap.mjs`). Its sole check is that the typed number is between 120 and 240. So a plan whose boxes total 105 EC is accepted if the student types 120. The mentor catches that by hand. **This is doing exactly what it was built to do — capture the choice and route it to the mentor — and it simply was not built to add up.**

In this case: Osiris"*) and as the place to register for Research Topics and the Final Project. It is the system of record for registration, and correct at that stage; composition of the 120 EC is a mentor decision made outside it.

# 9. Lund University

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

Lund is the most tooled-up Swedish target so far. Three things exist that Uppsala did not have:

1. **"Min studieplan" (My study plan) — official LTH, inside Kurswebben (kurser.lth.se).** LTH's own course-application help describes it as *"A new service/app [My Curriculum] that combines the curriculum of your programme with data from Ladok"* which *"summaries points (=help for control if one meets exam requirements)"*. Same system also shows *"blockers (which prevent from registration) for pre-requisitis or limitation of number of students"* — i.e. LTH already does a prerequisite block at registration time. Source: https://kurser.lth.se/kursanmalan/ . **Caveat: my evidence here is a single help page fetched without login; I could not get inside the tool. Treat "how much does Min studieplan actually validate" as the single biggest open risk in this target.** What it demonstrably is: a Ladok-backed summing of *completed* credits against the programme curriculum. What I found no evidence of: checking specialisering rules, or checking a *future* plan of not-yet-taken courses.
2. **studyplan.se — a third-party, student-built planner for LTH.** Built by three LTH students (Adrian Steene, Christoffer Sylve, Oliver Levay). Tracks credit totals, the 45 hp specialisation minimum, A-level credits, and computes *"which specialization is closest to fulfilling all requirements"*. Explicitly **not** official: no LTH partnership claimed, footer "2024 Studyplan", and its own pages carry the disclaimer *"lyckades inte hitta någon information om denna kurs"* (couldn't find information about this course) — incomplete data coverage. Sources: https://studyplan.se/information , https://www.studyplan.se/about . Read this as **evidence of demand, not as a solved problem**: three students built it because *"Som studenter stod vi inför utmaningen att planera vår specialisering"*.
3. **Ladok** — records results. Doing its job, at a later stage than this.

**The real approval gates at Lund (two, and neither is "an individual study plan approved at the start of the semester"):**

- **Degree-project eligibility check.** LTH prints it plainly: *"Before students can start their degree project the international coordinator of the programme (for master's students) or the programme planner (for MSc Engineering students) must check their eligibility."* The threshold: *"students must have completed a minimum of 240 credits within the relevant programme, including at least 30-second cycle credits"* and *"passed all compulsory courses within the basic block of the programme"*. Checked when the student submits the online degree-project form. Source: https://www.student.lth.se/english/masters-students/degree-project/engineering-msc/ — **this is the named human doing credit arithmetic per student. It is the Lund analogue of the Uppsala hook, and it is a harder check.**
- **Degree application (examensansökan).** No preliminary review is offered. The student self-certifies; for a civilingenjörsexamen they must *"ange din specialisering i rutan Meddelande till handläggare"* and a handläggare then checks. Source: https://www.student.lth.se/mina-studier/examen/

**Corrections to the brief:**

1. **"Before the individual study plan is approved" does not describe Lund.** There is no individual study plan for second-cycle students at LU/LTH. At LTH the phrase *individuell studieplan* is reserved for **third-cycle (PhD) students** — every hit for it is a doctoral regulation (phd.lth.se). For master's students LTH says the opposite: *"The courses that you will study initially at LTH are for most of our master's programmes already selected and added to your study plan"*, and the international coordinator only contacts you *"If you are required to make a choice for courses for study period 1"*. Source: https://www.lth.se/english/study-at-lth/masters-programmes-in-english/preparing-for-your-studies/
2. **For the civilingenjör programmes there is explicitly NO up-front check at all.** LTH states that no control of course selection happens when you pick a specialisation — it is the student's own responsibility, and the check happens only at degree application. This is a *stronger* wedge than an approval step, not a weaker one: the failure mode is discovered years late.
3. **"MSc Data Science" at the Faculty of Science does not exist.** Lund's Faculty of Science master's list has nine areas; there is no standalone Data Science programme. The nearest is **Computational Science (NABER)** with 8 specialisations (Physics, Geoscience, Scientific Computing, Life Sciences, plus Applied variants) and **Bioinformatics (NABIN)**. Source: https://www.science.lu.se/education/programmes-and-courses/masters-programmes
4. **"MSc Computer Science and Engineering" is not a 2-year MSc at LTH.** *Civilingenjörsutbildning i datateknik* is a **300 hp / 5-year** integrated programme whose English degree title is "Degree of Master of Science in Engineering, Computer Science and Engineering". Pitching it as a 120 hp master's will read as not having done the homework.
5. **LTH's specialisering rules are richer than the brief assumed — and one of them is genuinely nasty.** From Utbildningsplan Civilingenjörsutbildning i datateknik 24/25 (https://www.student.lth.se/fileadmin/lth/student/Utbildningsplaner_2023_och_fram/Grund_svenska/24_25/Utbildningsplan_Civilingenjoersutbildning_i_datateknik_24-25.pdf), exact:
   - *"Utbildningen innehåller en specialisering om minst 45 högskolepoäng, varav minst 30 är på A-nivå."*
   - *"Utbildningen får innehålla maximalt 15 högskolepoäng externt valfria kurser inkluderat LTH-gemensamma kurser."*
   - *"Utbildningen innehåller ett examensarbete om 30 högskolepoäng på A-nivå."*
   - *"För studenter antagna till kull 2023 eller tidigare innehåller utbildningen totalt 300 högskolepoäng varav minst 75 är på A-nivå."* / *"För studenter antagna till kull 2024 eller senare … varav minst 90 är på A-nivå."* — **the A-level requirement jumped 75 → 90 hp for the 2024 intake**, so two cohorts are being advised under different rules simultaneously.
   - *"Grundblock med obligatoriska kurser om 180 högskolepoäng varav minst 60 är på G2 eller A-nivå"*, and *"minst 27 högskolepoäng i matematik i grundblocket"*.
   - **The killer rule:** *"För att uppfylla examenskravet för en specialisering skall specialiseringskurserna ingå i en och samma läro- och timplan från studentens fjärde läsår eller senare."* — all specialisation courses must belong to **one and the same year's läro- och timplan**. Course-to-specialisation mapping changes between years, so a plan assembled from two different years' catalogues silently fails. This is exactly the class of constraint a human checker misses and a validator does not.
   - Also worth knowing: *"I examensbeviset anges inte genomförd specialisering"* — the specialisation is not printed on the certificate. It is a gate, not a badge. Do not pitch it as something the student shows off.
6. **Datateknik has 8 specialisations**, not a free-form elective space: Images and Graphics, Hardware-Near System Development, Embedded Systems, Machine Intelligence (AI), Networks and Security, Software, Software Engineering, Systems Signals and Control. Source: https://www.lth.se/utbildning/datateknik300/
7. **MLSC's real numbers** (Utbildningsplan, Master's Programme in Machine Learning, Systems and Control): 120 credits; *"Depending on the chosen track, the programme includes 30 or 37.5 credits of elective courses"*; degree project 30 credits; *"90 credits must be second-cycle credits, including the degree project."* Two alternative compulsory tracks (Machine Learning: EDAP01, FMAN45, FMSN50 / Systems and Control: FRTN60, FRTN70). Main field of study: Machine Learning, Systems and Control.
8. **Bioinformatics (NABIN) is more open than the brief implies:** degree project of *"30, 45, or 60 credits"* and *"up to 30 credits of completely free choice courses"* — a genuine combinatorial validation problem against the national 120 hp / advanced-level-depth rules. Source: https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/study/bioinformatics-master-programme-NABIN/programme-structure

---

**Verdict: SEND — but rank Lund BELOW Uppsala, and reframe the pitch.**

Send, because the degree-project eligibility check is a named human doing per-student credit arithmetic against a printed threshold, and because the civilingenjör side has an explicitly *unchecked* five-year course choice governed by six interacting numeric rules, one of which (same läro- och timplan) is nearly impossible to verify by eye.

Rank it below Uppsala, because unlike Uppsala, Lund is not tooling-free: it has an official Ladok-backed progress view, a registration-time prerequisite blocker, and a student-built specialisation planner already in circulation. The naive "nobody has any tooling" line will be factually wrong and will cost credibility. **Do not send that line to Lund.**

Not a deprioritise — this is not IST Lisbon. IST's in-house system actually validated plans; Lund's tooling sums *completed* credits from Ladok and blocks *registration*. Neither validates a *prospective* plan against specialisering rules. That gap is real and it is where the pitch goes.

**Hook:** *"the programme planner must check their eligibility"* — 240 credits, ≥30 second-cycle, full basic block, per student, per form, by hand. Plus, for the civilingenjör side: six numeric rules and a cohort-dependent A-level threshold that changed from 75 to 90 hp for kull 2024, with **no check at all** until the student applies to graduate.

# 10. University of Bonn

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**

BASIS (HISinOne) is Bonn's Studienportal. Per the Institut für Informatik's own BASIS page, it does exam registration (An-/Abmeldung), seminar/lab registration and self-service Transcript of Records generation. There is nothing on that page — or anywhere in the Prüfungsordnung — indicating BASIS checks a module selection against the Schwerpunkt minimums, the seminar/lab caps, or module Teilnahmevoraussetzungen. It is a registration and records system. It counts a sum, not a structure.
Source: https://www.informatik.uni-bonn.de/de/fuer-studierende/pruefungsanmeldungen/studienportal-basis

**No blanket study-plan approval.** PO 2023 § 4 Abs. 6: *"Für einen sachgerechten Aufbau des Studiums wird ein Studienplan als Empfehlung für die Studierenden aufgestellt. Der*Dem einzelnen Studierenden kann auf ihre*seine Anforderung hin ein individueller Studienverlaufsplan erstellt werden."* — the individual plan exists, is advisory, and is **opt-in on the student's own request**. Nobody has to submit one.

**But there IS a hard approval gate, and it is the one that matters.** PO 2023 § 4 Abs. 4, final sentence: *"Die Schwerpunktwahl kann vor Vergabe des Themas der Masterarbeit auf begründeten Antrag an den Prüfungsausschuss geändert werden."* The Schwerpunkt is **binding, not advisory**. Once the thesis topic is assigned it cannot be changed at all; before that it takes a reasoned application to the Prüfungsausschuss. Everything downstream of that single choice (the whole 88 LP Wahlpflichtbereich) is shaped by it — and nothing validates it at the moment it is made.
Source PDF: https://www.informatik.uni-bonn.de/de/studium/pruefungsamt/dokumente/po_master_2023.pdf/@@download/file/PO_Master_2023.pdf

Additional board powers (§ 8): the Prüfungsausschuss decides Anerkennungs-/Anrechnungsverfahren and can *"weitere Wahlpflichtmodule genehmigen"* (Anlage 1) — i.e. approve additional elective modules beyond the catalogue. Operationally that desk is Dr. Herman Haverkort's.

**Corrections to the brief — I got several things wrong:**

1. **"Structured area/Schwerpunkt system" is right; "structured" implying Pflichtmodule is wrong.** M.Sc. Computer Science has **zero taught compulsory modules**. The programme page says it outright: *"keine Pflichtmodule."* On that axis Bonn is closer to TU Berlin than I assumed, not further away.

2. **Unlike TU Berlin, the Wahlpflicht rules are hard, quantified and interlocking.** The real rule set for the 88 LP fachgebundener Wahlpflichtbereich (§ 4 Abs. 4 + Anlage 1):

   - 27–57 LP from the remaining three Schwerpunkte, with **at least two of those three at ≥ 6 LP each**
   - Global cap: **≤ 10 LP from Seminar-Modulen** across the whole Wahlpflichtbereich
   - Global cap: **≤ 18 LP from Lab-Modulen** across the whole Wahlpflichtbereich
   - Per-module Teilnahmevoraussetzungen (e.g. MA-INF 2212 Pattern Matching requires Computer Vision)
   That is six simultaneous constraints, and they genuinely conflict: seminar modules in Anlage 1 come at 4 **or** 6 LP, so **two 6 LP seminars (12 LP) already break the 10 LP cap** — while the Schwerpunkt separately forces you to take one. **Verdict: SEND — high confidence, one of the better-fitting targets in the campaign.**
