# 1. KU Leuven

**Incumbent tooling / approval process:**
KU Leuven runs a formal **ISP (Individueel Studieprogramma)** workflow inside **KU Loket**. Students compose the ISP, submit by the **third Wednesday of the academic year**, and **ISP coordinators assigned per programme "review, adjust, accept or reject your study programme and exemption requests"**; official approval lands **by 15 November at the latest**. Critically, the central page states programme rules **"are automatically checked when saving your ISP and you will receive a message if you did not follow them correctly."** So KU Leuven *already has* an in-system validator — any pitch that says "students can't check their plans" is factually wrong here.
Source: https://www.kuleuven.be/english/education/student/Learningpath/isp

Two hard limits of that incumbent tool, both stated by KU Leuven itself:

1. **It is single-year only.** The Master of Engineering: Computer Science ISP guidelines page says verbatim: *"The official ISP tool only allows you to select courses for the current year. However, it is good to plan ahead for the full programme, not just the first year. To facilitate this, we are working on a spreadsheet that students may fill in privately to prepare their official ISP."* That SharePoint spreadsheet (owned by programme director Dominique Devriese) is the actual incumbent for multi-year planning, and the page admits *"we try to keep it fully up to date"* — i.e. a maintenance burden they already feel.
Source: https://eng.kuleuven.be/en/study/masters/computer-science/isp-guidelines

2. **ISPs still get rejected for structural reasons.** The Master of AI FAQ lists the common rejection causes outright: *exceeding the maximum of 62 credits, incorrect elective course selection, missing mandatory components, empty ISP, close-to-complete but still incomplete ISP, insufficient credits for international students with a visa (min. 54 ECTS).*
Source: https://wms.cs.kuleuven.be/cs/studeren/master-artificial-intelligence/MAI_SIP/faq

Programme structure detail worth knowing (all verified):
- **Master of Engineering: Computer Science** (120 ECTS, reformed 2025-26 from 2 to **six options**: Distributed Systems, Secure Software, AI, Software Engineering, Human-Machine Communication, Computational Informatics). Per option: all compulsory courses **+ option electives to ≥18 ECTS**, with an overflow rule — *"If the total exceeds 18 ECTS, any extra credit will automatically be counted for Further Technical Elective Courses (for which you must also choose 18 ECTS)."* Plus **General Interest Courses 8–10 ECTS**, **broadening modules** whose content depends on the prior bachelor, an allow/deny list of cross-programme electives (MAI, Cybersecurity), and at least one incompatible-course pair. This is exactly the rule class a spreadsheet gets wrong.
- **Master of Artificial Intelligence** (advanced master, 60 ECTS, max 62 in ISP; options **ECS / SLT / BDA**, each with its own permitted elective list and conditional programming prerequisites).
- **Master of Electrical Engineering** (120 ECTS; four options **PSA / ECD / CC / ISSP**, each with an option coordinator). FAQ warns: *"You cannot graduate if you have not passed all courses from your ISP, even if you earned more than 120 credits. So be careful not to overload your ISP."*
Sources: https://eng.kuleuven.be/en/study/masters/computer-science ; https://eng.kuleuven.be/en/study/masters/electrical-engineering/electrical-engineering/students/faq ; https://eng.kuleuven.be/en/study/masters/electrical-engineering/electrical-engineering/contact

**Hook:**
KU Leuven's own CS programme page publicly admits the official ISP tool is single-year and that they are hand-maintaining a spreadsheet to cover the multi-year gap. StudyPlanner slots exactly there — **between the spreadsheet and KU Loket** — as the thing a student uses to arrive at a submittable ISP on the first attempt. Never frame it against KU Loket's own checks; frame it as removing the spreadsheet maintenance and the bounced-ISP rounds the MAI FAQ enumerates.

# 3. DTU

## Incumbent tooling / approval process

**DTU already has a study planner — and it explicitly does not validate.** This is the single most important finding, and it hands us the hook rather than blocking us.

**The tool:** `studieplan.dtu.dk` ("Study Planner"). It is the mandatory system for course registration and withdrawal. It lets students search courses, drag them into semesters, create placeholder activities (thesis, internship), check timetable clashes, and view ECTS distribution by category.

**What it does NOT do — verbatim from DTU's own IT guide:**

> "Remember that the Study Planner does not check for errors in your study plan. It is your responsibility to ensure that you include the correct courses in your study plan."
> — https://student.dtu.dk/en/help-and-guidance/it-guides-and-support/studieplanlaeggeren

> "If the registration is rejected, it may be because you are trying to register for a course that you are not permitted to take, either because it is not part of your study plan or because you exceed the number of permitted courses in a specific category or because the course is full and have no more available seats."
> — same page

So: category overflow is only discovered **at registration submission time, as a rejection**. There is no pre-submission validation of ECTS groups, prerequisites, or study-line completion anywhere in the DTU stack. That is precisely the gap StudyPlanner fills, upstream of both the Study Planner and the Head of Studies.

**Actual programme structure (IMPORTANT CORRECTION to the brief):**
DTU MSc Eng is **NOT** 30/30/30/30. Since September 2023 it is:

| Block | ECTS |
|---|---|
| Polytechnical Foundation (mandatory) | 10 |
| Programme-specific courses | 50 |
| Elective courses | 30 |
| Master's thesis | 30 (or 32.5 / 35) |
| **Total** | **120** |

The old 30 General Competence (GR) / 30 Technological Specialisation (TS) / 30 elective / 30 thesis split applies only to cohorts enrolled **before September 2023**. Do not pitch 30/30/30/30 — it dates you to the previous curriculum. Source: DTU Study Guidance, "Study Planning for New MSc Students", 8 July 2025, slide 11 — https://student.dtu.dk/-/media/websites/student/hjaelp-og-vejledning/studievejledningen/arrangementer/msc-study-planning-slides_e25.pdf

**Additional hard rules a validator can enforce (all verified):**
- Max **10 ECTS** of BSc-level courses in an MSc plan. BEng courses are **never** permitted — not even with an exemption from the Head of Studies or the Board of Exemptions (MMC spec).
- Thesis may commence only when the student lacks **no more than 15 ECTS** besides the thesis.
- All DTU MSc- and PhD-level courses are pre-approved; BSc courses pre-approved up to the 10 ECTS cap.
- A course becomes **binding** once a first exam attempt is used without withdrawal — even if it was an elective.
- Specialisations/study lines are "recommended ways of choosing courses". A student is admitted to the *programme*, not the line. **The line title only appears on the diploma if every requirement is fulfilled** — so a plan can hit 120 ECTS and still silently fail to complete the line the student thinks they are on. Strong validator use case.

**Who approves what:**
1. **Head of Studies (studieleder)** — approves deviations, substitutions (e.g. a GR/programme-specific course already passed during the BSc, where the HoS must designate a replacement), elective courses outside the curriculum, pre-approval of non-DTU courses, and the specialisation title on the diploma. The HoS then **emails the approval to the Office for Study Programmes and Student Affairs / Study Administration** — i.e. a manual, per-student, email-based workflow. This is the buyer.
2. **Board of Exemptions (CMDU)** — anything beyond the HoS's authority, via `dispensation.dtu.dk`.
3. **DTU Study Guidance (studievejledningen)** — front line for "study planning and rules, exemption, leave of absence, credit transfer".
