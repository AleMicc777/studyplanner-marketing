# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences on one page, evaluating on desktop during a workday:

- **MSc programme coordinators / study advisors** at European universities. They field the same plan-validation questions hundreds of times a term (EC shortfalls, unmet prerequisites, track violations) and catch real errors too late to fix. They want fewer emails and plans that arrive already correct.
- **University procurement, IT, and data-protection officers.** Skeptical evaluators who scrutinise claims, GDPR posture, deployment effort, and cost, and compare against peers (Stellic, Coursedog, EAB Navigate, Civitas).

Job to be done: decide whether to trust StudyPlanner enough to book a 20-minute demo and bring their programme guide.

## Product Purpose

StudyPlanner encodes an MSc programme's rules (mandatory courses, prerequisites, EC totals, track requirements) and validates every student's plan in real time, before it reaches a coordinator's inbox. The marketing site's single job is to convert coordinators and procurement into a booked demo, honestly: metric-led proof, a real clickable live planner, and an open founding-pilot offer. Success = qualified demo bookings and credibility that survives procurement scrutiny.

## Brand Personality

Confident, precise, committed. Bold, not startup-cute. The voice is plain-spoken and expert: it states the coordinator's problem in their own term-shaped words, avoids hype and fake precision, and treats the reader as a professional. Emotional goal on landing: "these people understand my exact problem, and I can trust them in front of procurement." Three words: **confident, precise, committed.**

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-slop landing pages: a page of identical hairline-bordered rectangles, flat hierarchy, no point of view.
- The current site's own failure: two blue systems at the same hue (near-black ink fighting a saturated brand blue). One committed identity replaces it.
- Startup-cute consumer SaaS: mascots, emoji, gradient text, decorative glassmorphism.
- Borrowed authority: fake university logos, fabricated customer quotes, invented precision, or any implication of a live TUM pilot that does not exist.
- Editorial-magazine affectation (display serif + italic + drop caps + broadsheet grid) applied because "landing pages do that."
- Reflex scaffolding: a tracked uppercase eyebrow above every section; 01/02/03 numbering unless the section is a real sequence.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** A tool that validates study plans must itself feel rigorous, exact, and correct. Precision is the product and the brand; sloppy design contradicts the pitch.
2. **Honest proof beats borrowed authority.** Lead with defensible metrics and a real, clickable live planner. Never a fake logo, quote, or unearned pilot claim. Hours-saved is always framed as an early estimate.
3. **Speak the coordinator's problem, not our feature list.** Open with the term-shaped pain in their language; earn the features afterward.
4. **Procurement-grade trust is a feature, not fine print.** EU hosting, data minimisation, cookieless analytics, and DPA readiness are surfaced as first-class content.
5. **One committed colour identity.** A single unified hue family carries 30-60%+ of the surface, with one locked accent, audited across every section. No hedging neutrals around the edges.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum sitewide; AAA contrast on the hero.
- OKLCH throughout. Verify body text at least 4.5:1, large text at least 3:1, placeholder text at least 4.5:1 against its actual background.
- Every animation has a full `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` alternative. Reveal animations enhance already-visible content; visibility is never gated on JS or intersection.
- Keyboard-operable nav, accordions, and forms with visible focus states. Colour is never the sole signal: plan validation uses icon plus text, not green/red alone.
